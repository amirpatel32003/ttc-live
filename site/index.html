How to read this

  • Live map. Your browser asks the TTC's public vehicle feed for positions every 15 seconds. Nothing on the map passes through a server of mine.
  • Speed is the speed each vehicle reported at that instant, averaged over vehicles that were moving. It is lower than a timetable speed because it includes slowing for stops and lights.
  • Bunched means two vehicles on the same route and direction within 300 metres of each other, both moving. Vehicles parked together at a terminal are not counted. Bunching usually means a long gap somewhere else on the route.
  • Gaps. For the route you pick, each vehicle is placed along the line by matching it to the route's stops. The longest stretch with no vehicle is the biggest gap. The wait shown is that distance divided by how fast the route is moving right now, so it is an estimate, not a prediction. Vehicles more than 400 metres off the line, such as replacement buses on a detour, are left out.
  • Near me lists vehicles within one kilometre of you and whether each is heading your way, judged from its compass heading.
  • A typical day comes from a collector that records the feed every 30 seconds for most of each hour and rolls it up by route and hour. It started in November 2026, so early figures rest on few days and will settle as more are added.
  • Report card, best time to leave, compare and roughest routes all come from the collected history, not the live feed. They show a route's usual pattern, not a prediction for today. Routes need a few hundred reports, and the weekly ranking needs three days of data, before they appear.
  • Subway trains are not in this feed. For those, see the subway delay dashboard.

Use the data

  • route_hour.csv: one row per route, date and hour with report counts, speed sums and bunching counts.
  • history.json: the hourly profile the chart uses.
